# FuturaMAX Deep Research: What airMAX AC-Class Hardware Can Still Do

## Executive assessment

**Bottom line:** FuturaMAX is technically credible, but the evidence supports a narrower—and more interesting—thesis than “replace old airMAX firmware and unlock a huge capacity multiplier.” The strongest evidence says that **substantial improvements remain possible in queueing latency, airtime fairness, interference-aware network configuration, telemetry-driven control, aggregation behavior, distance timing, and possibly supervisory rate control**. The evidence is much weaker that a host-side Linux stack can replace Ubiquiti’s proprietary scheduled airMAX MAC on QCA988x and beat it in dense outdoor PtMP. QCA988x is a firmware-offloaded architecture: Linux/mac80211 prepares queues and configuration, but HTT sends the data path into firmware and WMI sends control commands into firmware; Qualcomm explicitly describes the QCA9880 as having an onboard processor for low-level WLAN PHY/RF work. citeturn21search5turn8search24

The target hardware is nevertheless unusually attractive for research because **the LiteBeam 5AC Gen2 and NanoStation AC/Loco family are known OpenWrt-capable AR9342 + QCA988X systems**, and OpenWrt logs from Ubiquiti QCA988x hardware show ath10k-ct firmware features including `ratemask-CT`, `txrate-CT`, `tx-rc-CT`, `cust-stats-CT`, and `wmi-block-ack-CT`. LiteBeam 5AC Gen2 is documented with only 64 MiB RAM; related LiteAP AC designs use a single-core AR9342 with roughly 64 MiB RAM and 16 MiB flash, so FuturaMAX should assume severe embedded resource constraints until every LAP-GPS board revision is physically inventoried. citeturn17search10turn19search0turn19search6turn20search1

For the exact requested product set, the chipset evidence is strongest for **LiteBeam 5AC Gen2, NanoStation AC/Loco, and the LiteAP AC lineage**. OpenWrt identifies LiteBeam 5AC Gen2 as AR9342 + Qualcomm/Atheros QCA988X and identifies NanoStation AC Loco similarly; its LiteAP AC hardware data identifies QCA988x. Ubiquiti’s current LAP-GPS specifications confirm the GPS receiver, 5 GHz airMAX AC mode, distance adjustment, “Ackless Mode,” “Dynamic Ack,” traffic shaping and QoS, but Ubiquiti does not publicly identify the radio IC on that product page. I therefore classify **LAP-GPS = QCA988x as INFERRED from the LiteAP AC platform lineage, not independently CONFIRMED for every LAP-GPS revision**. citeturn17search10turn19search0turn19search6turn28search5

The most important architectural warning is that a large fraction of the classic research literature solves a problem **airMAX already solved**. WiLDNet, hMAC, Det-WiFi, OpenTDMF and related systems show that replacing uncontrolled CSMA with scheduled transmissions can produce dramatic improvements on hidden-node, long-distance, or deterministic networks. But an airMAX AC sector is already using a proprietary TDMA/TDD-style MAC. Those results prove that scheduling is valuable; **they do not prove that an OpenWrt/ath10k replacement would outperform airMAX scheduling**. WiLDNet obtained 2–5× throughput improvements over conventional 802.11 and as much as an eightfold difference in one 65 km bidirectional test; hMAC more than doubled aggregate throughput relative to a crude node-level TDMA baseline in one hidden-node experiment; Det-WiFi cut worst-case latency by roughly 9.5–15× relative to 802.11s. All three depended on substantially more host control over the MAC than stock QCA988x ath10k provides. citeturn25search0turn25search2turn10view2turn13view2turn26search0

Conversely, some of the most encouraging work does **not** require replacing the MAC. PNOFA changed A-MPDU decisions above proprietary Qualcomm firmware on a commercial IPQ4019 802.11ac AP and averaged **17% more throughput, with gains up to 29%**, in randomized real-world mobile trials without modifying the firmware. That is highly relevant to FuturaMAX because it suggests that useful optimization can exist even when much of an 802.11ac radio is offloaded—provided the host can obtain the necessary Block ACK/rate information and can influence aggregation. Whether QCA988x/airMAX exposes enough of those interfaces is one of the highest-value physical experiments. citeturn10view0turn12view0turn13view0

A second strong line of evidence concerns **buffering and airtime scheduling**. “Ending the Anomaly” moved per-station FQ-CoDel-style queues into mac80211 and demonstrated order-of-magnitude latency improvements under load and, in a 30-client independent test, throughput increasing from **3.3 to 17.7 Mbps** when airtime fairness prevented one slow station from dominating. Crucially, however, the authors could implement their airtime scheduler on ath9k but **not on ath10k because ath10k lacked the required scheduling hooks**. The later Airtime Queue Limits work was explicitly ported to mac80211/ath10k and produced a “very significant reduction in latency” in congested tests, although the initial test thread also reported accounting underflow bugs. citeturn12view7turn13view3turn13view4turn15view0turn15view1

The strongest target-specific opportunities I found are therefore **spectral telemetry, distance/ACK timing, aggregate queue control, slow-timescale rate supervision, per-peer statistics and network-wide configuration**. QCA988x has hardware FFT reporting exposed through ath10k spectral scan; Linux supports a QCA988x-specific coverage-class mechanism that rewrites MAC slot/ACK/CTS timing registers despite firmware resistance; and QCA988x 10.2.4 gained per-peer rate/retry/drop statistics using firmware pktlog events, albeit only roughly one event per four PPDUs. citeturn21search0turn22search2turn23search1

The strongest blocker is equally clear. Stock QCA988x firmware does not give Linux a faithful per-packet transmit-rate completion. The ath10k documentation historically reports 6 Mbps because transmit completions lacked TX-rate information; the later QCA988x workaround obtains sampled peer statistics from pktlog rather than the actual completion associated with every packet. A 2018 attempt to populate `ieee80211_tx_status()` explicitly warned that the supplied rate was from an earlier frame and that richer peer-rate support existed mainly in later QCA families. This makes NeuRA/IteRate-style per-frame host control much harder to transplant faithfully. citeturn7search2turn23search1turn23search5

My overall evidence grade is therefore:

**CONFIRMED on target hardware:** QCA988x supports ath10k, HTT/WMI offload, spectral FFT, limited manual rate masks, a coverage-class timing hack, sampled per-peer TX statistics, OpenWrt on several target-family Ubiquiti products, and enhanced CT firmware interfaces. citeturn21search1turn21search0turn6search2turn22search2turn23search1turn17search10turn20search1

**DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware:** adaptive aggregation can beat proprietary Qualcomm 802.11ac firmware; neural/statistical rate selection can beat Minstrel; host queueing/AQL can dramatically reduce Wi-Fi latency; software TDMA/polling can solve hidden-node and long-distance pathologies; and centralized optimization can outperform default contention parameters. citeturn13view0turn13view1turn13view4turn25search2turn26search0turn27academia39

**INFERRED:** a FuturaMAX controller using long-lived CPE history, spectral scans, sector topology, rate/retry statistics, distance, channel/width/power planning and Linux queue controls should improve some real sectors, particularly badly tuned or interference-limited ones. The magnitude cannot be credibly predicted before physical experiments. citeturn21search0turn23search1turn5search0

**UNKNOWN:** whether target airMAX firmware exposes useful Block ACK/aggregation controls; whether CT firmware is sufficiently stable for production PtMP; whether Linux can reproduce airMAX’s proprietary scheduler and GPS behavior; and whether the LAP-GPS radio revision is identical enough to the documented LiteAP QCA988x platform for every low-level technique. citeturn8search4turn20search1turn28search5

**REFUTED as a general proposition:** “because commodity Wi-Fi research obtained large gains over CSMA, replacing airMAX with OpenWrt should obtain those gains.” The baseline is wrong: airMAX already uses scheduled TDD/TDMA, whereas most spectacular academic results compare with DCF/802.11s or intentionally unfair queues. citeturn25search2turn13view4turn26search0


## Hardware and software control boundary

The critical fact for FuturaMAX is not merely that QCA988x is supported by Linux. It is **where decisions actually live**.

| Layer | What is demonstrably controllable | What remains below/opaque | FuturaMAX implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| External/network controller | Channel, width, transmit power, fixed-frame/TDD configuration where exposed, traffic shaping, sector coordination, historical models | Individual PPDU timing and immediate retransmission decisions | **High-confidence research surface.** Slow optimization needs no PHY modification. Ubiquiti currently documents channel/frequency, traffic shaping, distance adjustment and related controls on LAP-GPS. citeturn28search5 |
| Linux/qdisc | FQ-CoDel/CAKE-style traffic isolation, pacing and shaping before Wi-Fi queues | Firmware-internal queue residence and MAC retry delays | Strong for **latency/isolation**, not by itself a radio-capacity multiplier. The Wi-Fi queue research shows that queue placement matters enormously. citeturn12view7turn15view1 |
| mac80211 | TXQs, per-station queueing, AQL accounting, BA session control framework, rate-control framework on drivers that expose it | Cannot override decisions that ath10k firmware has absorbed | Useful, but ath10k is not equivalent to ath9k: “Ending the Anomaly” specifically lacked ath10k scheduling hooks for the airtime scheduler. citeturn12view7 |
| ath10k host driver | WMI configuration, HTT submission, spectral scan, debug/pktlog stats, bitrate masks, QCA988x coverage-class register workaround | Most fine-grained PHY/MAC policy | **Key experimental boundary.** Data frames go through HTT; configuration commands go through WMI. citeturn21search5turn21search0turn22search2 |
| QCA firmware | Rate/retry handling, significant transmit scheduling/aggregation behavior, firmware queues, low-level device policy | Source and exact internal algorithms are generally unavailable | Main obstacle to custom rate/retry/aggregation and microsecond scheduling. The absence of true TX-rate completions is direct evidence of this ownership. citeturn7search2turn23search3 |
| QCA988x hardware | PHY/RF, CCA, MAC timers, FFT engine, descriptor/ring machinery | Hardwired features and undocumented registers | Some unusual capabilities can be reached: QCA988x coverage class works by rewriting MAC timing registers, while FFT samples are generated by the baseband. citeturn22search2turn21search0 |
| Proprietary airMAX components | Fixed/flexible framing, GPS synchronization, proprietary airMAX AC MAC, dynamic/ackless distance behavior | Scheduler implementation, slot-allocation policy, exact rate/retry/aggregation integration, possible DSP/firmware acceleration | **Largest unknown.** Ubiquiti exposes behavior but not enough internals to assume Linux can reproduce it. citeturn28search3turn28search5 |

The HTT/WMI distinction is fundamental. ath10k’s own architecture document says frames are sent through **HTT** while configuration is sent through **WMI**. That is a very different programming environment from ath9k, where research prototypes could manipulate contention windows, diagnostic registers, queue stops and hardware behavior directly from the driver. citeturn21search5turn26search0

There is an important exception: **QCA988x is old enough that several low-level MAC registers are still directly reachable from the host.** The 2016 coverage-class patch exists because stock Qualcomm firmware would not implement the requested function; Linux developers instead rewrote slot-time and ACK/CTS timeout registers and re-applied the values after firmware events. The developers explicitly called the solution fragile and said a firmware implementation would be preferable. This proves that the offload wall is not absolute—but it also demonstrates how adversarial firmware/register ownership can become. Direct URL: https://lists.infradead.org/pipermail/ath10k/2016-August/008286.html citeturn22search2turn22search6

Manual rate control is similarly partial. QCA988x ath10k accepts VHT bitrate masks, but the documented masks are constrained: VHT MCS sets are effectively contiguous groups such as 0–7, 0–8 or 0–9 rather than an arbitrary retry chain. Thus a controller can restrict the firmware’s search space or sometimes force narrow selections; that is not equivalent to owning the PPDU-by-PPDU retry series. Direct URL: https://wireless.docs.kernel.org/en/latest/en/users/drivers/ath10k/debug.html citeturn6search2

The telemetry boundary is better than the original ath10k documentation suggests, but still imperfect. Qualcomm’s 10.2.4 branch for QCA988X did not return TX rate in ordinary completions; a later driver patch used firmware pktlog to obtain per-peer successful packets/bytes, drops, retries and PPDU rate information. The firmware emits roughly **one pktlog event per four PPDUs per peer**, so this is potentially excellent for a seconds-to-hundreds-of-milliseconds historical controller but not a faithful per-packet control loop. It was tested on **QCA9880 + 10.2.4.70.48**. Direct URL: https://lists.infradead.org/pipermail/ath10k/2017-December/010529.html citeturn23search1

ath10k-ct expands that surface. A current OpenWrt boot log from Ubiquiti QCA988x hardware reports CT 10.1 firmware features including `ratemask-CT`, `txrate-CT`, `tx-rc-CT`, `cust-stats-CT`, `retry-gt2-CT`, `txrate2-CT`, and `wmi-block-ack-CT`. This is unusually relevant to FuturaMAX because those names map almost directly onto several requested research areas. They prove that **a modified QCA988x firmware can expose more control/telemetry than stock firmware**; they do not prove that those facilities are stable enough or sufficient to construct airMAX-quality PtMP scheduling. citeturn20search1

Candela’s situation is mixed. The open ath10k-ct driver repository remains visibly maintained/rebased across modern kernel versions, including directories through Linux 6.18, but the corresponding firmware streams are old and the 10.2.4 page itself warns of known bugs and recommends 10.1 for some QCA988x use. OpenWrt discussions have also noted that new firmware feature development was effectively not planned. Direct URLs: https://github.com/greearb/ath10k-ct and https://www.candelatech.com/ath10k-10.2.php. citeturn23search0turn8search4turn8search17

A final target-specific asset is the FFT engine. ath10k exposes hardware spectral samples as TLVs, with **64, 128 or 256 FFT bins**. Background scanning can operate on the current channel while the device is not busy. But the interface has known defects: configured sample counts may be ignored on 20/40 MHz, and 64-bin sampling at 80 MHz returns bogus data and is discarded. That makes the feature suitable for slow interference classification, not a hard-real-time interference oracle without extensive validation. Direct URL: https://wireless.docs.kernel.org/en/latest/en/users/drivers/ath10k/spectral.html citeturn21search0


## Strongest positive evidence

The following are the twenty findings I would treat as the strongest evidence *for* FuturaMAX. They are ordered roughly by relevance and evidentiary strength, not by headline percentage.

1. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — QCA988x provides usable FFT/spectral data.** Baseband FFT samples are exposed under ath10k, making interference classification and network-wide channel selection possible without a new PHY. The known 20/40 MHz counting bug must be engineered around. Layer: **hardware + ath10k + userspace/controller**. citeturn21search0

2. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — host software can alter long-distance timing.** The QCA988x coverage-class implementation modifies slot time plus ACK/CTS timeout registers, despite the stock firmware not supporting the operation properly. This is direct evidence that long-distance ACK timing is not completely immutable. Layer: **ath10k + hardware registers**, with firmware interference. citeturn22search0turn22search2

3. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — per-peer rate/retry/drop statistics are recoverable.** On QCA9880 firmware 10.2.4.70.48, pktlog provides successful bytes/packets, drops, retries and rate data approximately once per four PPDUs. That is enough for historical CPE modeling at slower control timescales. Layer: **QCA firmware → ath10k → controller**. citeturn23search1

4. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — CT firmware exposes richer knobs than stock firmware.** Ubiquiti QCA988x OpenWrt logs show CT rate masks, TX-rate controls, custom stats and WMI Block ACK features. This is the strongest evidence that FuturaMAX can experimentally widen the WMI boundary without designing firmware from scratch on day one. Layer: **ath10k-ct + CT firmware**. citeturn20search1

5. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — PNOFA improves proprietary-offload 802.11ac aggregation without firmware modification.** Google Wifi/IPQ4019 AP, Microsoft Surface client, office/NLOS movement, saturated UDP; ten randomized interleaved **30 s** trials per algorithm. PNOFA beat the proprietary Qualcomm aggregation algorithm by **up to 29%, mean 17%**. Layer: **userspace/driver-visible BA + aggregation policy**. Direct PDF: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~brecht/papers/pnofa-mswim-2020.pdf. citeturn13view0turn12view0

6. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — PNOFA can approach an offline optimum.** Its trace evaluation put the algorithm close to the statistically optimal aggregation policy across varied scenarios, showing that BA-history-based aggregation contains exploitable information rather than simply tuning a static maximum. Portability turns almost entirely on whether QCA988x can expose BA outcomes and an aggregation knob with sufficiently low delay. citeturn10view0

7. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — host Wi-Fi queue architecture can dominate latency and aggregate performance.** “Ending the Anomaly” changed mac80211/ath9k/ath10k queueing, and the 30-station independent reproduction increased aggregate throughput from **3.3 Mbps to 17.7 Mbps** under the intentionally pathological slow-station case. Mean latency across stations improved about **2×**. Layer: **mac80211/driver**. citeturn13view4

8. **DEMONSTRATED with ath10k — AQL directly targets excessive firmware/driver airtime backlog.** The mac80211 AQL work was ported from an ath10k implementation; ath10k testing reported a “very significant” latency reduction under congestion. This directly supports FuturaMAX experimentation with queue-depth-in-airtime rather than packets/bytes. Layer: **mac80211 + ath10k**. citeturn15view1turn15view0

9. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — per-link scheduling cures severe hidden-node starvation.** hMAC used two APs and three stations with AR9280/ath9k. In its hidden-node experiment, ordinary DCF left one link effectively starved; classical node TDMA produced **4.2 Mbit/s aggregate**, while hMAC’s per-link schedule produced **8.8 Mbit/s**. Error bars were standard errors. Layer: **host queue gating/SoftMAC-style scheduler**. citeturn13view2turn12view4

10. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — long-range links benefit enormously from scheduling and propagation-aware recovery.** WiLDNet used commodity Atheros cards and a Click shim on real links up to **65 km**, replacing CSMA behavior with TDMA plus bulk ACK/FEC. In the 65 km Ghana test, simultaneous bidirectional TCP improved from **0.68 ± 0.39 to 5.51 ± 0.07 Mbps**, ten 30 s measurements at 11 Mbps PHY. Layer: **host shim + modified MAC behavior**. citeturn25search2

11. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — deterministic TDMA is feasible entirely on commodity 802.11 silicon where the driver has MAC-register control.** Det-WiFi used **AR9287 + ath9k, Ubuntu 14.04, kernel 3.14.57**, explicitly disabled NAV/CCA/backoff behavior and used 600 µs slots. It ran in a real industrial RF environment, five repetitions of ten minutes each. citeturn26search0

12. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — Det-WiFi dramatically tightened latency distribution.** At 500-byte payloads, two-hop maximum latency was about **1.394 ms versus 21.2 ms** for 802.11s; four-hop maximum was **2.861 ms versus 27.3 ms**. The tradeoff is important: Det-WiFi recorded roughly **0.22–0.58% loss where the 802.11s test recorded zero**. This is evidence for determinism, not an unconditional win. citeturn26search0

13. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — sophisticated rate selection can beat Minstrel in real hardware, not only simulation.** NeuRA’s ath9k prototype produced **14% and 16% higher average throughput** than Minstrel HT in two stationary tests, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals. It used a TL-WDN4800 AP and TL-WDN4200 client, saturated UDP, and randomized interleaved trials; baseline Minstrel throughput was 58.3 and 159.7 Mbps in the two setups. Layer: **host rate control**. Direct PDF: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~brecht/papers/neura-mswim-2020.pdf. citeturn13view1turn11view1

14. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — a modest embedded CPU can execute useful ML rate logic.** NeuRA’s inference loop at 1 ms used at most roughly **20% of one AMD Phenom II core**, and moving to 5 ms reduced CPU use to around 12% without measured throughput loss in its prototype. This does not prove AR9342 feasibility, but it shows that an off-box controller or coarser embedded loop need not be computationally exotic. citeturn10view1

15. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — newer automated in-kernel rate-control research still finds meaningful headroom.** The May 4, 2026 IteRate preprint used a **58-node testbed and five workloads** and reports, against Minstrel, **21% faster web loads, 7% higher video QoE and 21% higher peak throughput**. Its key enabling feature is per-frame MCS/retry telemetry exported to eBPF—the exact facility QCA988x does *not* cleanly expose, making IteRate more important as a statement of potential than as portable code. Direct URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02542. citeturn27academia36

16. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — joint rate/aggregation/MIMO adaptation can outperform narrower rate-only control.** SampleLite adapted the set of 802.11n link features jointly, reported more than **70% lower sampling overhead than Minstrel HT** and **35–100% higher goodput than existing schemes** across controlled and real interference experiments. Its portability is conceptual because ath10k firmware owns much of this joint decision space. citeturn27search6

17. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — centralized contention optimization has real-hardware value.** A control-theoretic study implemented centralized and distributed adaptation on **18 commercial devices** and found substantial throughput gains under varying station counts, link qualities and traffic; centralized control performed most consistently. This supports FuturaMAX’s premise that fixed topology and network-wide knowledge are exploitable advantages. Direct URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.2970. citeturn27academia39

18. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — airtime rather than packet fairness is the right fairness objective in heterogeneous-rate PtMP.** The independent 30-station reproduction in “Ending the Anomaly” measured airtime estimates against independent packet captures and found only about **1.5% average discrepancy**. That is strong evidence that practical airtime accounting can be accurate enough for a scheduler. citeturn13view4

19. **CONFIRMED in proprietary airMAX — GPS/fixed TDD timing can materially reduce self-interference when sectors are geometrically compatible.** Ubiquiti added GPS Sync to airMAX AC in airOS 8.3; fixed mode requires synchronized frame duration and UL/DL structure. A third-party co-channel test with 22 km and 3 km Rocket Prism links reported unsynchronized average latency around **26–38 ms** with modulation collapsing toward 16-QAM, versus **10–12 ms** and 256-QAM after 5 ms fixed-frame GPS sync. This is not peer-reviewed and is not on LAP-GPS, so it is supporting field evidence only. citeturn28search11turn28search4

20. **CONFIRMED in airMAX product behavior — older hardware already contains useful software-defined distance and traffic controls.** Ubiquiti currently lists **Ackless Mode, Dynamic Ack, distance adjustment, WMM/user priority and burst-capable traffic shaping** for LAP-GPS. Those are existing control surfaces that FuturaMAX can characterize before attempting any firmware replacement. Layer: **proprietary airMAX component**. citeturn28search5


## Strongest blockers, regressions, and refutations

The following twenty points are at least as important as the positive evidence.

1. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — per-packet TX completion telemetry is deficient.** QCA988x historically returns no actual TX rate in ordinary completions, producing the famous hard-coded 6 Mbps display problem. This directly obstructs host-side per-frame rate/retry algorithms. citeturn7search2

2. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — the later telemetry workaround is sampled and delayed.** QCA988x pktlog gives one event roughly every four PPDUs; a later `tx_status()` proposal warned that the rate attached to a completion could actually be from a previous frame. A controller that assumes exact action→result correspondence will learn the wrong model. citeturn23search1turn23search5

3. **CONFIRMED — the core MAC is firmware-offloaded.** HTT transports frame descriptors into firmware while WMI transports configuration. That makes a QCA988x ath10k device fundamentally less programmable than the ath9k hardware used by hMAC, Det-WiFi and most successful commodity TDMA work. citeturn21search5turn26search0

4. **CONFIRMED — the most successful mac80211 airtime scheduler originally could not run on ath10k.** “Ending the Anomaly” implemented its scheduler on ath9k specifically because ath10k lacked the required scheduling hooks. Queueing improvements did transfer; direct scheduler control did not. citeturn12view7

5. **CONFIRMED on target hardware — rate masks are not arbitrary retry programs.** QCA988x manual VHT bitrate configuration accepts constrained MCS ranges, not a freely programmable sequence of rates/retry counts. NeuRA/EDRA-style action spaces therefore cannot simply be transplanted through `iw`. citeturn6search2

6. **CONFIRMED — long-distance timing control required a fragile register hack because Qualcomm would not implement it in stock firmware.** The coverage-class developers explicitly had to monitor firmware resets of registers and rewrite them after WMI events; later review called the mechanism fragile. That is a warning about every proposal based on fighting firmware ownership. citeturn22search0turn22search6

7. **CONFIRMED — ath10k firmware can suffer state degradation that the host driver cannot repair elegantly.** A QCA9880 report described throughput falling from roughly 50 Mbps to 5 Mbps after days; an ath10k developer suspected firmware/rate-control state and suggested a firmware restart because the driver could do little else. This is anecdotal but target-chip-specific negative evidence. citeturn23search3

8. **CONFIRMED — CT firmware is not simply “better Qualcomm firmware.”** Candela’s QCA988x 10.2.4 page explicitly says it has known bugs and recommends 10.1 in some deployments. CT issues also document firmware crashes, severe latency spikes and feature regressions on related ath10k hardware. citeturn8search4turn8search21turn8search9

9. **CONFIRMED on Ubiquiti/OpenWrt — platform integration can regress independently of the radio.** OpenWrt 24.10 bug reports on multiple Ubiquiti devices showed broken writable overlays; a NanoStation AC Loco user separately reported JFFS2 corruption-like behavior after self-built 23.05/24.10 images. A research firmware must solve flash/update/recovery reliability before radio experimentation is meaningful. citeturn20search1turn20search0

10. **CONFIRMED — the embedded host is small.** LiteBeam 5AC Gen2 is a roughly 64 MiB-class AR9342 system, and related LiteAP AC data shows a single 533 MHz-class MIPS core, 64 MiB RAM and 16 MiB flash. Large ML frameworks, detailed packet histories and heavy userspace telemetry belong off-box or in very compact implementations. citeturn17search10turn19search10

11. **DEMONSTRATED on adjacent hardware — userspace scheduling loses determinism under load.** hMAC’s authors observed netlink scheduling messages being delayed/dropped enough to lengthen or shorten slots and recommended **>10 ms slots plus guards** unless slot timing moved into the kernel/hardware. That is incompatible with treating a userspace daemon as a microsecond TDMA scheduler. citeturn10view2

12. **DEMONSTRATED — disabling CSMA precisely required ath9k-specific register access.** Det-WiFi set AR9287 diagnostic flags to ignore virtual carrier sense/force RX clear and set contention windows to zero. There is no demonstrated equivalent control surface for stock QCA988x firmware in this research set. Therefore a literal port is **blocked until proven otherwise**. citeturn26search0

13. **REFUTED — spectacular DCF-to-TDMA gains are not evidence of gains over airMAX.** WiLDNet’s 2–5× result, Det-WiFi’s latency reductions and hMAC’s hidden-node gains all compare with ordinary 802.11 or crude TDMA baselines. airMAX is already scheduled. Treating those percentages as available FuturaMAX headroom would be methodologically wrong. citeturn25search0turn26search0turn13view2

14. **DEMONSTRATED — fairness can deliberately hurt a disadvantaged subscriber.** In the 30-client airtime-fair experiment, overall throughput and average latency improved dramatically, but latency at the intentionally slow station became roughly an order of magnitude worse. “Fairness” has to be tied to subscriber plan/SLA semantics rather than blindly equal airtime. citeturn13view4

15. **DEMONSTRATED — AQL’s first ath10k tests exposed accounting bugs.** The reported latency improvement was accompanied by occasional underflow of total pending airtime. That is not evidence against AQL today, but it is a reminder that airtime estimates and firmware completions must be validated on the exact firmware branch. citeturn15view0

16. **CONFIRMED — spectral scan is imperfect on the target generation.** Sample-count commands can be ignored on 20/40 MHz, and one 80 MHz bin configuration is unusable. Continuous background scanning also only runs when the radio is not busy, creating sampling bias exactly when a sector is heavily loaded. citeturn21search0

17. **UNKNOWN/likely blocked — stock firmware does not expose a documented arbitrary A-MPDU-length API equivalent to PNOFA’s requirement.** PNOFA is therefore not “portable because both devices are Qualcomm.” It is portable only if FuturaMAX can retrieve BA outcomes and force/limit aggregate lengths on the actual QCA988x firmware. The CT `wmi-block-ack-CT` feature is encouraging but does not by itself prove that control. citeturn13view0turn20search1

18. **UNKNOWN/likely blocked — proprietary airMAX GPS/TDD scheduling is not part of upstream ath10k/OpenWrt.** Ubiquiti’s releases describe fixed/flexible framing and GPS Sync as airMAX features, while upstream ath10k describes standard mac80211/HTT/WMI interfaces. Flashing OpenWrt should therefore be assumed to sacrifice airMAX scheduled MAC/GPS behavior unless independently demonstrated otherwise. citeturn28search3turn21search5

19. **CONFIRMED field risk — proprietary airOS itself is not necessarily stable.** A 2025 field report described repeated kernel panics on **25 LiteAP GPS units plus Rocket AC devices** running airOS 8.7.15, with a crash in the proprietary `umac` receive path. It is only a user report, not a reproduced root cause, but it demonstrates that “stay proprietary” is not automatically a zero-risk baseline. citeturn28search0

20. **UNKNOWN — no credible published experiment found here demonstrates OpenWrt/ath10k on these exact CPE/AP models beating airMAX AC TDMA in a many-CPE outdoor sector.** This is the central missing piece. It should be treated as a negative evidence gap, not silently filled by adjacent Wi-Fi papers.

The resulting custom-MAC judgment is severe but not absolute: **a full new microsecond PtMP MAC above stock QCA988x firmware is likely blocked; a hybrid controller that controls Linux queues, restricts firmware choices, harvests telemetry and performs slow network-wide optimization is much more plausible.** The historical SoftMAC/Det-WiFi/hMAC evidence shows what is possible when MAC control exists; the QCA988x evidence shows why that control cannot be assumed. citeturn26search0turn10view2turn21search5

**Ranked transfer candidates**

**First — Network-wide channel/width/power/GPS-TDD optimization: CONFIRMED control surface, INFERRED gain.** This is the least firmware-dependent approach and makes maximal use of FuturaMAX’s unusually valuable prior information: fixed topology, known distances, sector geometry and historical interference. It should operate at seconds/minutes/hours rather than packet timescales. Spectral FFT plus per-CPE statistics are sufficient inputs. citeturn21search0turn23search1turn28search5

**Second — AQL + modern per-station queueing + appropriate subscriber shaping: DEMONSTRATED on ath10k/mac80211 for latency.** This is likely the highest-confidence way to improve loaded latency and reduce firmware queue buildup. Subscriber plans of 10–20 Mbps make controlled pacing particularly attractive because traffic can be shaped before a giant radio queue forms. It should not be sold internally as an RF-capacity technique. citeturn15view0turn15view1turn13view4

**Third — Per-CPE slow historical controller: CONFIRMED telemetry, INFERRED benefit.** Feed RSSI/SNR, retries, pktlog rates, throughput, queue delay, FFT context, distance and time-of-day history into an off-box controller. Use it to constrain rather than replace firmware decisions. Fixed CPEs make this far more plausible than the mobile Wi-Fi problem studied by most rate-control papers. citeturn23search1turn27academia36

**Fourth — Adaptive aggregation: DEMONSTRATED adjacent and unusually relevant.** PNOFA is the strongest direct proof that proprietary 802.11ac firmware can leave aggregation performance on the table. The project should prioritize discovering `wmi-block-ack-CT` semantics and whether A-MPDU limits can be changed per peer/traffic state. citeturn13view0turn20search1

**Fifth — Coverage-class/distance timing calibration: CONFIRMED on QCA988x.** Because subscriber distances are stationary, FuturaMAX can avoid the generic WLAN problem of changing propagation delays. Test whether airMAX’s Dynamic ACK already sits at the optimum; if not, per-link or sector-aware timing may recover unnecessary timeout/retry overhead. citeturn22search2turn28search5

**Sixth — Firmware-rate-search supervision rather than full host RA: CONFIRMED partial control.** Use rate masks or CT controls to exclude chronically bad NSS/MCS choices for a stationary CPE, then let firmware perform microsecond retry decisions. This is much more realistic than replacing firmware RA with Minstrel/NeuRA. citeturn6search2turn20search1

**Seventh — Interference classifier from FFT + ordinary counters: CONFIRMED telemetry, INFERRED policy.** Classify persistent narrowband interference, adjacent-channel energy and temporal occupancy; use that to drive channel/width/power decisions rather than attempting per-frame avoidance. Hardware shortcomings in spectral sampling are manageable at this timescale. citeturn21search0

**Eighth — Topology-aware airtime quotas/fairness: DEMONSTRATED adjacent.** Subscriber plans, distance and estimated airtime cost can produce a better objective than naïve equal throughput or equal packets. The key unknown is whether stock/CT ath10k offers sufficient dequeue authority on QCA988x to enforce it directly, or whether only upstream pacing can approximate it. citeturn13view4turn12view7

**Ninth — Selective RTS/CTS/hidden-node policy: INFERRED.** The literature overwhelmingly confirms hidden-node harm, but I did not find target-QCA988x PtMP measurements demonstrating a particular RTS threshold is superior to airMAX scheduling. This belongs in experiments, not the initial architecture. hMAC and WiLDNet show the cost of unresolved hidden nodes, not that RTS/CTS itself is the optimum remedy. citeturn13view2turn25search2

**Tenth — Full custom PtMP TDMA: likely blocked.** It becomes rational only if experiments discover undocumented/CT firmware interfaces that can stop firmware transmissions, schedule exact peer windows and control retry/aggregation state with bounded timing. The available evidence does not establish that capability. citeturn21search5turn26search0

OpenFWWF and older SoftMAC work are important historically because they show that “commodity hardware” does not imply “fixed MAC.” But their success depended on firmware/driver architectures that were open enough to move timing-critical behavior. That distinction is exactly why they should not be treated as proof that QCA988x is equally programmable.

**Patents and proprietary boundaries.** The most clearly relevant Ubiquiti patent family found is **US9191037B2, “Wireless radio system optimization by persistent spectrum analysis,”** priority October 11, 2013, granted November 17, 2015, with later continuation activity. It describes persistent spectrum analysis and is directly adjacent to any FuturaMAX network-wide interference-classification work. Direct URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US9191037B2/en. citeturn5search0

A second relevant family is **US10057021B2, “Resource allocation in a wireless communication system,”** priority April 24, 2015. It is the closest Ubiquiti patent located in this investigation to proprietary wireless scheduling/resource-allocation behavior. Direct URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US10057021B2/en. citeturn4search16

Ubiquiti also has long-range radio-system patents including **US9496620B2** and related **US9972912B2**, useful when mapping hardware/system architectural claims around long-range links. Direct URLs: https://patents.google.com/patent/US9496620B2/en and https://patents.google.com/patent/US9972912B2/en. citeturn4search24

I did **not** independently verify a Ubiquiti patent whose primary-source title/claims should confidently be summarized as “the propagation-aware airMAX TDMA scheduling patent.” The seed is plausible, and the resource-allocation families deserve claim-by-claim legal/technical review, but FuturaMAX should not treat that informal description as established fact. This is one of the places where the research found less certainty than the seed list implied.

Ubiquiti’s public releases establish the proprietary functional boundary more clearly than the patents do. airOS 8.3 added **GPS Sync to airMAX AC** on June 16, 2017; later releases added fixed/flexible TDD framing variants and capped some GPS/flexible modes at **60 stations**. On GPS-equipped devices, fixed-frame selection can automatically enable GPS synchronization. Those mechanisms are therefore software-defined features of airMAX, but their scheduler code and detailed algorithms are not part of upstream mac80211/ath10k. citeturn28search11turn28search3turn28search1

Qualcomm’s proprietary boundary sits one layer lower. The QCA9880 contains an onboard CPU intended to offload low-level WLAN/PHY-RF operation from the host. Upstream ath10k distributes firmware binaries through several API generations; for QCA988X, the mature 10.2/10.2.4 firmware family dates from the mid-2010s. New Linux driver code can improve queueing, accounting and WMI handling, but it cannot automatically replace algorithms compiled into those firmware binaries. citeturn8search24turn7search1

**The ten physical experiments with the highest information value are:**

1. **AirMAX-versus-OpenWrt sector A/B.** Same AP, same antenna, same 15–30 stationary CPEs, same channel/power/width, randomized firmware order. Measure aggregate TCP/UDP, offered-load sweep, per-CPE 99th-percentile latency, airtime, retries, fairness, and recovery after interference. This single experiment answers whether abandoning proprietary TDMA is even a rational branch.

2. **Exact target-chip inventory.** Read PCI IDs, board/subsystem IDs, calibration partitions, CPU/RAM/flash and firmware identifiers from LAP-GPS, LiteBeam 5AC Gen2, NS-5AC and Loco5AC hardware revisions. The project should not proceed on “QCA988x-class” assumptions where a board revision can invalidate register hacks.

3. **Block ACK observability/control test.** On CT 10.1 and stock firmware, determine whether every BA bitmap—or a statistically useful sample—is available per CPE, its delay relative to transmission, and whether aggregate-length limits can be changed without restarting a peer. This decides whether PNOFA-class work is genuinely portable.

4. **TX telemetry truth experiment.** Capture over the air with an independent monitor while recording HTT/pktlog/`iw`/CT stats. Quantify how accurately host telemetry reconstructs actual PPDU MCS/NSS, retries and timestamps. Without this, every ML/historical controller risks learning mislabeled outcomes.

5. **AQL/TXQ stress test on exact QCA988x firmware.** 30–60 CPEs, realistic 10/20 Mbps shaped plans, mixed upload/download/VoIP, deliberate overload. Sweep AQL and queue limits and compare median/95th/99th latency, throughput and firmware crashes. This has one of the highest probabilities of yielding a real production benefit.

6. **Distance-timing sweep.** CPEs at several controlled path delays or real distances, test automatic/dynamic ACK against fixed coverage class/ACK timing. Measure retransmissions, aggregate duration, goodput and latency. This determines whether airMAX already extracts all available propagation-timing gain.

7. **Rate-mask supervisory-control test.** For stationary near/far links, compare firmware auto RA against conservative and history-derived MCS/NSS masks under changing interference. A positive result would establish a useful middle ground between firmware RA and impossible per-frame host RA.

8. **Hidden-node matrix.** Construct known hidden-node and near/far pairs. Compare airMAX TDMA, OpenWrt CSMA, RTS/CTS thresholds, host pacing and—if feasible—scheduled queue gating. Measure not only aggregate throughput but starvation probability and tail latency. This is essential because DCF literature cannot substitute for an airMAX baseline.

9. **Spectral-scan validity/overhead test.** Generate known Wi-Fi and non-Wi-Fi interferers; compare QCA988x FFT against an external spectrum analyzer. Measure classification accuracy, scan bias during high load, CPU cost, throughput loss and whether 20/40 MHz sample-count anomalies matter in practice.

10. **GPS/frequency-reuse experiment with PtMP CPEs rather than PtP links.** Two or more back-to-back/adjacent sectors, same and adjacent channels, controlled antenna isolation. Sweep fixed frame size and DL/UL ratio. This is the only way to determine whether an external FuturaMAX optimizer can improve Ubiquiti’s static GPS plan in the intended topology. Ubiquiti’s own documentation makes clear that GPS/fixed framing is sensitive to compatible frame duration and UL/DL ratio, while field evidence shows geometry can make or break reuse. citeturn28search3turn28search4

The source trail leads to one central research conclusion: **the unexploited value in QCA988x is more likely to be above and around the proprietary/offloaded MAC than inside a wholesale replacement for it.** Target hardware demonstrably exposes spectral measurements, timing registers, peer statistics, Linux queueing and, with CT firmware, additional rate/Block-ACK-related controls. Adjacent-hardware research repeatedly shows that those classes of information can produce real gains. At the same time, the exact facilities required for a new airMAX-class scheduler—deterministic peer-level transmission ownership, arbitrary retry policy, exact per-frame completion telemetry, explicit aggregation control and firmware-queue visibility—are precisely the areas where QCA988x is least open. citeturn21search0turn22search2turn23search1turn20search1turn21search5

Accordingly, the highest-value FuturaMAX evidence program is not “write a new MAC first.” It is to **measure the offload boundary on the exact radios, preserve airMAX as the benchmark, and determine experimentally how much authority remains at three interfaces: Linux queueing, WMI/CT firmware controls, and network-wide slow control.** A positive answer at even two of those three levels would justify substantial further engineering; failure to expose Block ACK/aggregation/rate controls, combined with an inability for OpenWrt to approach airMAX TDMA under hidden-node load, would strongly argue against a custom-firmware branch before large development costs are incurred. citeturn13view0turn13view4turn20search1turn25search2
