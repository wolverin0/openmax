**Executive assessment**

Credible, reproducible evidence exists that newer software layers (mac80211 AQL/airtime fairness, improved aggregation control, rate adaptation, spectral tools, and Candela ath10k-ct firmware) can deliver material gains in latency, fairness, and single-link or modest multi-station throughput on QCA988x-class 802.11ac hardware under OpenWrt. Gains are typically measured in controlled or semi-controlled settings and are largest for latency under load and multi-rate fairness.

Matching or exceeding proprietary airMAX AC TDMA performance in the target fixed outdoor WISP PtMP environment (many stations per sector, hidden nodes, near/far disparity, real interference, 10–20 Mbps plans, stationary CPEs) is not supported by current evidence and faces hard firmware-offload barriers. Full custom scheduled MAC / pure TDMA on firmware-offloaded QCA988x is largely blocked. Older SoftMAC / MadWifi / FreeBSD TDMA techniques (ath9k-era and earlier) do not transfer cleanly. Many promising ideas remain simulation-only, abandoned, or demonstrated only on adjacent hardware (ath9k, Intel, IPQ4019 Wave-2, etc.).

The highest-probability path for FuturaMAX is incremental cross-layer optimization _within_ the existing ath10k + mac80211 + Linux stack (AQL + airtime fairness + aggregation tuning + rate masks + spectral feedback + external controller), not replacement of the airMAX MAC. Physical experiments on real LiteAP GPS / LiteBeam / NanoStation / Loco 5AC hardware are required to quantify residual headroom.

**The 20 strongest positive findings**

  1. **AQL + airtime fairness (“Ending the Anomaly”, 2017)** – Order-of-magnitude latency reduction under load, large multi-station throughput gains, near-perfect airtime fairness for TCP/UDP. Implemented in mac80211 + ath10k. Demonstrated on QCA hardware. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent + target-class.
  2. **AQL-FQ-CoDel on ath10k** – Mean queue depth and in-flight airtime collapse (e.g., 1197→37 frames, 203→6.3 ms airtime) while holding throughput; 98th-percentile latency ~1000 ms → <100 ms. DEMONSTRATED.
  3. **PNOFA aggregation (2020)** – Practical near-optimal A-MPDU length using Block ACK delivery ratios. Up to 29 % (mean 17 %) UDP throughput gain vs Qualcomm proprietary aggregator on Google Wifi (IPQ4019). User-space, no firmware change. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent Wave-2.
  4. **ath10k-ct firmware + driver** – Extra features (rate masks, multi-VIF, RX software crypt, safer burst, better stats, station-kickout control). Widely used in OpenWrt; actively maintained by Candela. CONFIRMED usable on QCA988x.
  5. **Spectral FFT on ath10k** – Full FFT reports (64/128/256 bins) available via debugfs; usable for interference classification. CONFIRMED on QCA988x.
  6. **WiLDNet (NSDI 2007)** – 2–5× TCP/UDP throughput and lower loss on long-distance links via TDMA-like changes + FEC/bulk ACK on commodity cards. DEMONSTRATED on older hardware.
  7. **FreeBSD ath TDMA (Sam Leffler / RCP)** – Hardware-assisted TDMA on Atheros chips (AR5416+). Deployed in real long-distance networks. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent (pre-ac).
  8. **hMAC (2016)** – Hybrid TDMA/CSMA on ath9k SoftMAC using power-save queue control; open-source. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent.
  9. **Det-WiFi (2017)** – Multihop TDMA on COTS 802.11 for deterministic industrial use; better latency/jitter than 802.11s. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent.
  10. **SoftMAC / OpenFWWF / MadWifi experiments** – Feasibility of disabling CSMA elements and implementing custom MAC on pre-ac Atheros. Foundational. DEMONSTRATED historically.
  11. **Coverage-class / ACK-timeout patches** – Possible via WMI on some CT / 10.4 firmwares or register hacks; critical for multi-km links. PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED.
  12. **Minstrel-HT baseline + later RL variants (EDRA, IteRate)** – Minstrel remains default; recent AI/RL rate control shows 7–21 % application gains or higher in specific tests. DEMONSTRATED / recent.
  13. **GPS Sync design (Ubiquiti)** – Co-located APs transmit/receive simultaneously → frequency reuse, lower self-interference. CONFIRMED on target LAP-GPS hardware.
  14. **Airtime fairness scheduler migrations into mac80211** – Deficit round-robin preferred over virtual-time for stability with AQL on ath10k. CONFIRMED in OpenWrt trees.
  15. **Peer/TID configuration and rate-mask extensions in CT** – Host can influence more rates than stock firmware. DEMONSTRATED.
  16. **FQ-CoDel / CAKE + pacing** – Standard Linux tools reduce bufferbloat when combined with AQL. DEMONSTRATED broadly.
  17. **Historical models / cross-layer control** – Per-CPE history + external controller feasible on the Linux side. INFERRED viable.
  18. **Block ACK / A-MPDU telemetry via HTT** – Enough visibility for user-space aggregation optimizers (PNOFA-style). DEMONSTRATED.
  19. **OpenWrt latency fixes for QCA988x** – Specific patches reduced >100 ms latency bugs. CONFIRMED.
  20. **Real outdoor long-distance experience** – WiLDNet, FreeBSD TDMA, and various WISP reports show software TDMA/scheduling can outperform plain CSMA on commodity hardware when distances and hidden nodes dominate. DEMONSTRATED on adjacent.


**The 20 strongest blockers / negative findings**

  1. Heavy firmware offload of rate control, aggregation, BA windows, and TX scheduling on QCA988x (WMI_SERVICE_RATECTRL and related).
  2. Incomplete / delayed TX-completion and airtime telemetry from firmware → AQL and fairness schedulers operate on estimates.
  3. Hidden firmware queues that mac80211 cannot fully drain or re-order.
  4. No SoftMAC equivalent for ath10k; full custom MAC requires firmware changes (closed or NDA-limited).
  5. CT firmware still cannot implement true airMAX-style fixed-frame TDMA with GPS-grade slotting.
  6. OpenWrt / ath10k PtMP frequently underperforms proprietary scheduled MACs under high station counts + hidden nodes (community reports and design analyses).
  7. Device resource limits (LiteAP GPS: ~64 MB RAM, modest CPU) constrain complex ML or per-CPE models.
  8. Unstable or limited rate masks on stock firmware.
  9. Aggregation controls incomplete; host cannot fully dictate A-MPDU size/policy in all paths.
  10. Coverage-class / long-distance ACK timing only partially exposed and hardware-dependent.
  11. Many academic TDMA systems (SoftMAC, MadMAC, early FreeBSD) abandoned or never ported beyond ath9k.
  12. Results that exist only in simulation or small indoor testbeds.
  13. Near/far and hidden-node problems remain severe under CSMA even with RTS/CTS and AQL.
  14. GPS Sync and fixed framing are proprietary and tightly coupled to airOS.
  15. Proprietary airMAX scheduling patents and closed source create legal/IP boundaries for direct re-implementation.
  16. ath10k push vs pull modes interact poorly with some fairness schedulers (documented latency/burstiness).
  17. Spectral scan useful for classification but not real-time closed-loop control without significant host processing.
  18. Negative community reports of OpenWrt on NanoBeam AC / similar showing signal instability or throughput regression vs stock.
  19. RL/ML rate control (EDRA, IteRate) still early; overhead and generalization to outdoor PtMP unproven.
  20. Inability to match airMAX’s dynamic airtime redistribution and priority under mixed 10–20 Mbps plans without owning the scheduler.

**Ranked list of techniques most likely to transfer to FuturaMAX**

  1. AQL + mac80211 airtime fairness + FQ-CoDel/CAKE (already works).
  2. ath10k-ct firmware + rate-mask / peer-config extensions.
  3. User-space or host-side aggregation optimizers (PNOFA-style) using Block ACK telemetry.
  4. Spectral FFT → interference classification → channel/width/power decisions (external controller).
  5. Improved Minstrel-HT or lightweight RL rate adaptation with outdoor-specific training.
  6. Coverage-class / ACK-timeout tuning for known distances.
  7. Per-CPE historical models + cross-layer controller (Linux side).
  8. RTS/CTS, CCA, ANI tuning under real interference.
  9. GPS-disciplined external timing for multi-sector coordination (where hardware allows).
  10. Pacing and queue discipline at the IP layer.


**Techniques likely blocked on QCA988x**

  * True fixed-frame TDMA with microsecond GPS-grade slotting and dynamic airtime redistribution matching airMAX.
  * Full SoftMAC / arbitrary MAC protocol replacement.
  * Complete host ownership of aggregation policy and retry chains.
  * Fine-grained per-MPDU TX-completion for ideal AQL.
  * Hardware-accelerated propagation-aware scheduling.


**Important source-code repositories and maintenance status**

  * ath10k-ct (greearb/Candela) – actively maintained; OpenWrt packages current.
  * Linux ath10k (mainline) – maintained.
  * mac80211 AQL/airtime patches – upstream + OpenWrt.
  * FreeBSD ath TDMA – historical; not actively ported to ac.
  * hMAC (szehl/ath9k-hmac) – archived / inactive.
  * Older SoftMAC / OpenFWWF / MadWifi – abandoned.
  * Ubiquiti GPL releases – incomplete / historically problematic; airMAX TDMA core closed.


**Patents and proprietary boundaries**

Ubiquiti holds patents and trade secrets around airMAX TDMA, GPS Sync frame synchronization, and related scheduling (public design guides describe simultaneous TX/RX for co-located sectors). GPS Sync is free of extra licensing cost on supported hardware but the implementation is closed. Direct re-implementation of the exact airMAX scheduler risks IP issues; higher-layer optimizations and open techniques do not.

**Ten highest-value questions only physical experiments can answer**

  1. On identical LiteAP GPS + Loco/NanoStation 5AC hardware, what is the capacity, latency, and fairness of optimized OpenWrt/ath10k-ct + AQL vs stock airMAX AC under 20–50+ CPEs with realistic near/far and hidden-node geometry?
  2. How much residual gain remains from PNOFA-style aggregation once AQL and airtime fairness are already enabled?
  3. Can CT rate masks + improved Minstrel/RL close most of the rate-adaptation gap to airMAX under outdoor fading and interference?
  4. What is the practical limit of host-side spectral classification + multi-sector channel/power optimization on the available CPU/RAM?
  5. How accurate is firmware-reported airtime vs true over-the-air airtime under heavy aggregation and retries?
  6. Does enabling coverage-class / ACK-timeout extensions produce stable multi-km PtMP without regressions?
  7. At what station count does CSMA + AQL collapse relative to fixed-frame TDMA?
  8. Can an external GPS-disciplined controller coordinate multiple OpenWrt sectors usefully, or is firmware-level sync required?
  9. What is the CPU/memory overhead of per-CPE historical models + closed-loop control on a 64 MB LiteAP-class device?
  10. Do any combinations of the above produce _net_ better subscriber experience (latency + fairness + stability) at 10–20 Mbps plans than current airMAX, or only marginal single-link gains?
